About me

I’m Anwar Khan, but almost everyone calls me Ani. I’m a senior at Atholton High School in Columbia, Maryland, Class of 2027. I build things, I photograph things, and I spend more time than I should thinking about how cars work.

Most of my projects start the same way: I want something I can’t afford, or something that doesn’t exist yet, so I try to make it. My six-degree-of-freedom motion simulator started because commercial rigs cost more than a used car. The handbrake, the seatbelt tensioner and the sim dash all came out of the same rig. The go-kart started as a rusty KT100 that wouldn’t idle. None of them went right the first time, and the build journals on the SPF Maker Club site are honest about that.

In January 2023 I took over SPF Maker Club from my sisters, who started it. Since then it’s been my lab notebook: CAD screenshots, calculations, failed prints, and what I learned each time. I also co-founded the Atholton Electronics Club, where I lead the mechanical side of Aqua Guardian, our solar-powered water-quality buoy that made the Samsung Solve for Tomorrow national semifinals. We were one of only two teams from Maryland, and we won $10,000 for our school.

This year I’m an engineering intern at AnTrust in Columbia through the HCPSS Gifted & Talented Research Program. I work on precision measurement with a Keyence IM-8000, writing measurement routines, improving inspection reports and documenting how the machine should be used, so a measurement means the same thing no matter who takes it.

Photography is the other half of me. I took photography classes all through high school, and I shoot cars at the track, cities at night, and anything my friends will stand still for. I like composites too: double exposures, cause-and-effect diptychs and the occasional surreal chessboard. Those are on the Photography page, ordered best first.

Outside of all that, I play soccer (number 18 for Atholton), I sim race, and I flag at Dominion Raceway when I can. I also keep my lab cleaner than the rest of the house. OCD may not be diagnosed, but I can’t stand a mess. And there’s Loki, my black cat, who thinks every project is a bed.

Growing up making things

Long before the Maker Club, I was the kid taking things apart at the kitchen table. In elementary school, my Odyssey of the Mind team built clown cars that actually had to drive, and we took 2nd place at state in 2019. That was the first time I learned that the build is only half the job; the other half is making it survive the trip to competition.

In fifth grade my friend Matt and I built an air-quality logger for the science fair that measured VOCs and CO₂ around the house. I made gliders and boomerangs, learned to solder from a time-lapse I recorded myself, and helped my sister Gabby wash and prep her Supra for her graduation. Looking back, every one of those projects is somewhere inside what I build now.

Teams and competitions

I’ve always liked building with other people more than alone. My first robotics team, the Maker Faire table where I explained my projects, and the Electronics Club bench have all taught me the same lesson: the best ideas usually come from the person who sees the problem differently than you.

Karting, cars and race weekends

Cars are the thread through almost everything I do. I started at indoor kart tracks, rebuilt my own KT100 kart, flag at Dominion Raceway, and spent a day with my dad at the Porsche Experience Center.

Soccer

Soccer has been part of my week for as long as I can remember. I play for Atholton in number 18. It’s where I learned to keep going when I’m tired, and breaking my femur at an ID camp made me miss it more than I expected.

Loki

Loki is my black cat and my most patient model. He supervises every build and sleeps on whatever I need next.
