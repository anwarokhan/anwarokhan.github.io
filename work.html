Mechanical design, controls and FEA · December 2024 to now

6DoF Motion Simulator

The biggest thing I have built. I wanted my racing simulator to actually move, so I started designing a motion platform under it. The first layout was a pentapod with bent connecting rods. When a quote came back at $114 per rod, I redesigned it as a compact hexapod with straight members.

Along the way I ran FEA on the arms and frame, worked out the inverse kinematics and motor torque math in my own design workbook, wired the drives and power distribution, and built a full-scale model with printed gearboxes to debug homing, direction and geometry before buying final parts. The aluminum-extrusion cockpit is assembled, and I am now moving toward a more compact three-axis platform.

Team project with the Atholton Electronics Club · 2025 to now

Aqua Guardian

Our Electronics Club’s floating water-quality station: it runs on solar power, samples the water it sits in, and uploads what it measures so anyone can check it. I co-founded the club and lead its mechanical team, so the hull, mounts, panels and enclosure are mine to figure out with the team. The steep A-frame solar panels keep it powered as it turns on its anchor. A foam ring at the waterline keeps it upright in wind. A teammate learning heat simulations caught that my antenna mount was hurting the signal, and we redesigned it together. We tested it through the ice in February, made the Samsung Solve for Tomorrow national semifinals as one of two Maryland teams (winning $10,000 for our school), and just got approval to launch in Lake Elkhorn.

Raspberry Pi, Linux and 3D printing · 2025 to 2026

Sim Racing Dash

I needed a dashboard in front of me for RPMs, and the ones online cost $100 or more. I had a Raspberry Pi 3B+ and a small touchscreen lying around, so I designed a case that mounts to my Moza wheel base and posted it on Thingiverse.

Getting the software right took longer than the case. After a white screen I could not SSH into and Chromium refusing to open in kiosk mode, I rebuilt it on Pi OS Lite. Now it boots, joins Wi-Fi and opens a SimHub dashboard from my PC on its own. I wrote the whole setup down as a guide.

Engine repair and fabrication · 2025

Go-Kart Restoration

A Yamaha KT100 Swiss Hutless kart that had sat for years. It would only run for four seconds on starter fluid. Taking the carburetor apart, my dad and I found a worn pump diaphragm that would not pulse.

After that came a homemade kill switch (the factory harness was missing, and the kart nearly went through the garage door), a smaller split sprocket so it would not drag on the asphalt, a rusted axle, and yellow Dark Knight stripes. Then, finally, the first real drive.

Mechanical and embedded · 2024 to 2025

Seatbelt Tensioner

Motors behind the seat wind the harness straps tighter as the car brakes, using telemetry from the game through SimHub. I designed the housings, a 3:1 reduction and a center buckle, and wrote the control code.

The first version burned out its drivers a few minutes into a session, with smoke coming out of the controller box. I rebuilt the drive side and soldered my own power board to carry the current the motors actually pull.
